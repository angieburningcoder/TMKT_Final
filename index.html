老師的 7 個提醒 → 這頁每一段都對應到

  • 1RQ1、RQ2 是情境分析、不是問題：透過私下訪問幾位飼主了解「為什麼大家不知道這個品牌？是不需要、還是覺得別牌更好？為什麼更好？」 → 11 飼主訪談
  • 2RQ3（買／訂閱少）是衍生問題：知道的人少 → 自然沒人買 → 07 問題樹
  • 3SMART 要量化：「三個月內從多少 → 多少」，例如 FB／IG follower 數 → 08 SMART
  • 47P 只選一個，底下可有多個 tactics，執行細節要講清楚 → 09
  • 515 分鐘只講 1–2 個核心 tactic，重點是「說服別人投資你」 → 01 時間條
  • 6成本要算：找誰、多少錢（媒合平台有網紅行情）、人選合理嗎（有養寵物的人，例：蔡依林有養狗） → 10 預算
  • 7只選一個市場：美國靠次級資料；台灣可以直接訪問消費者 → ✓ 已定案台灣

Group Project – Marketing Plan（老師規格書全文）

原檔：Final_project_instruction.pdf（科技行銷 Final project instruction, updated）。此為文字版，方便閱讀與給 AI 讀取；如有出入以 PDF 為準。

Main tasks

As a marketing consultant, your group is required to develop a three-month Marketing Plan for a selected firm or organisation. You may choose:

  • a technology firm or technology-related organisation that interests your group;
  • a company where one or more group members are currently working, interning, or have previously worked or interned; or
  • a self-created or newly established business, provided that sufficient market information is available for meaningful analysis.

The purpose of this project is not simply to describe the company or discuss a marketing case. Instead, your group is expected to develop a practical and evidence-based marketing plan that could realistically be implemented.

Your group should first conduct a situation analysis to understand the company's current marketing environment. This may include analysis of its customers, competitors, internal strengths and weaknesses, and relevant external market factors.

Based on your situation analysis, you should identify one main marketing problem or challenge that the company is currently facing. The identified problem should be clearly supported by your analysis and appropriate evidence.

You should then develop a three-month marketing plan to address the identified problem. Your plan should include clear marketing objectives, appropriate strategies, feasible marketing actions, and measurable outcomes.

Be creative and make the written report professional and reader-friendly.

Notes

  • If you choose a company where you currently work, intern, or have previous work experience, your first-hand knowledge may be used as supporting insight. However, personal experience does not replace the requirement to conduct a situation analysis or provide appropriate supporting evidence.
  • If your group selects a company where a member works or interns, you should not disclose any confidential, sensitive, or proprietary company information.
  • If you choose a self-created or newly established business, you are still required to support your analysis with relevant market, customer, competitor, and industry information. Assumptions should be clearly identified and justified.

Company selection guideline

Technology firms and technology-related industries are strongly encouraged. However, companies from other industries may also be considered, provided that sufficient information is available to conduct a meaningful marketing analysis and develop a feasible marketing plan.

Final project process（課堂投影片）

Company selection → Situation analysis → Identify one main problem → SMART objective → Marketing strategy → Tactics → Implementation & evaluation

Recommended structure and format for marketing plan

You can include, but you are not limited to, the following components. Due to word limits, you may not be able to include all the bullet points or components listed below. Some of the listed components may not be relevant to your specific analysis. It's important to select the components that best fit your analysis. For instance, a situation analysis involves various aspects, and you might choose to concentrate on one or two key aspects rather than providing a comprehensive overview of all elements. This focused approach allows for an in-depth analysis and the presentation of valuable insights, potentially resulting in higher marks. Furthermore, to maintain focus, discussing one main problem is sufficient. However, you can have multiple objectives to address that problem.

Executive summary

  • The executive summary should fit into a paragraph.
  • A brief summary of the main goals and recommendations of the plan, helping readers find the plan's major points quickly.
  • The executive summary should capture the soul of the plan. That is, the reader should be able to know what the entire plan is about, without reading every single section.

1. The selected firm/organisation profile

  • Company background (industry, size, operations).
  • Digital business models of the company.
  • Description of the firm's product or service portfolio.

2. Situation analysis

2.1 Customer analysis

  • Based on what criteria did you segment the whole market, such as…
  • demographic criteria (e.g., age, income, etc.)
  • psychographic criteria (e.g., lifestyle, interests, etc.)
  • geographic criteria (e.g., customers from different regions or countries, etc.)
  • how customers used our products or services (e.g., customer's frequency of purchases)
  • Select and analyse a target segment and argue for it as the most visible and profitable target customers.
  • Analyse and discuss the target segment's experiences.

2.2 SWOT — Strengths. Weaknesses. Opportunities. Threats.

2.3 Competitive analysis

  • Conduct a brief competitive analysis for the company in the selected target segment.
  • The analysis should be on 1 or 2 key competitors.
  • Impact of the internet on different competitive forces in the selected target segment.

2.4 Macro-environment analysis — Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental forces.

3. Identify the main problem and SMART objectives for your marketing plan

  • Identify the main problem based on the situation analysis.
  • Identify the main reasons leading to the main problem.
  • Define objectives and marketing goals (i.e., increasing sales volume, ROI), with SMART criteria (please provide justifications for each of the SMART criteria, e.g., why and how you choose or decide on each criterion?).

SMART: Specific (clear and specific) · Measurable (quantifiable) · Achievable (realistic) · Relevant (align with the main problem) · Time-bound (specific deadline).

4. Specific marketing strategies

  • Define customer persona.
  • Formulate the 1–2 specific marketing strategies (e.g., product, price, place, and promotion, people, process, and physical evidence) you plan to implement to achieve the objectives.

5. Evaluation and selection of marketing tactics

In this section, you are expected to explore and evaluate possible marketing tactics to address the key problems identified earlier:

  • Propose 2 or 3 potential marketing tactics that could help solve the main problem.
  • Analyse the pros and cons of each tactic.
  • Assess the feasibility of each tactic by evaluating whether it can be realistically implemented, based on your available time, manpower, technology, and financial resources.
  • Based on your analysis, and considering real-world constraints (limited time, manpower, and resources), recommend the most feasible and appropriate marketing tactic(s).

6. Implementation and action plans

  • Spells out specific implementation of these marketing strategies, including: What will be done? When will it be done? Who will do it? How much will it cost (i.e., estimated cost)? Will the company need to recruit new talents to implement the plan? Will the company need to hire external agencies or partners?
  • Identify which marketing channels to use for the campaign and assess the suitability of different channels for your designed campaign.
  • Craft key activities that the company should undertake within the plan's timeframe.

7. Evaluation of your marketing strategies and campaigns

  • Estimate the potential returns from the proposed marketing strategies and campaigns. Include projections on when the campaign might reach the break-even point, considering costs and expected revenue.
  • Define key milestones to track progress during the implementation phase. These serve as checkpoints to assess whether the campaign is on track to meet its objectives.
  • Outline the types of data that will be collected during the campaign (e.g., website analytics, social media engagement, sales figures, customer feedback). Describe how this data will be used to evaluate the effectiveness of the strategies.

9. Appendix (if applicable)

You are free to include an appendix; however, no mark is allocated to the information contained in the appendix.

Manage group dynamics

You are expected to actively manage meeting conduct, record members' contributions, and document the decisions and actions agreed upon at each meeting. Clearly documented procedures and agreed-upon outcomes are essential professional tools when working in groups. Each student within the group will receive the same mark unless there is a dispute regarding an individual's contribution.

Conflict and grievance procedures

Groups function optimally when all members actively participate in task development and resolution. However, there may be situations where one or more members do not attend meetings or complete their work. In the first instance, such issues should be addressed within your group. If a grievance issue arises and cannot be resolved within the group, the next step is to discuss the situation with the lecturer. Based on the evidence provided, the lecturer will determine whether a single group mark is warranted or if individual marks should be awarded. Please retain your meeting notes, drafts, and email communication in case there is any dispute.

Rules for using AI tools

You are allowed to use ChatGPT and other AI tools; however, you must clearly disclose their use and provide a brief learning report that includes the name of the AI tool(s) used, their specific purpose (e.g., grammar checks, concept clarification), and how they supported your learning. Additionally, you should include a critical analysis of the AI-generated content, evaluating its strengths, limitations, and how you verified, revised, or applied it to your work.

It is strictly prohibited to directly copy and paste AI-generated content as your own work, as this constitutes a breach of academic integrity. Instead, you must critically evaluate and revise the content so that your final work reflects your own understanding, judgment, and independent thought. Submitting AI-generated content without proper disclosure or presenting it as your own work may lead to penalties in accordance with academic integrity policies.
